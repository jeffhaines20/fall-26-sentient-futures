# Review of `TEAM-BRIEF.md` — faithfulness and outsider readability

Two rounds. The brief was checked against the three source files it summarises, by a critic
told to assume a smart reader with no ML background who has read nothing else.

## Round 1 — faithfulness 6/10, readability 7/10

**Faithfulness problems (all fixed):**

1. **2A's participant count and cost were actually 4A's numbers.** The brief said ~325
   participants and ~$1,400–1,800; 2A's own table says ~160 for one item class, ~477 for all
   three, ~$700–900. **The single worst error — it attached one project's budget to another.**
2. **Reinstated a description the sources had explicitly retracted.** *Choose Your Agent*
   described as giving "a choice between AI assistance modes"; the sources say each game
   grants one modality and the choice is use-vs-not.
3. **Paired a meta-analysis headline N with a result from a much smaller subset** — the exact
   conflation an earlier critique had already corrected in the source (depression rests on
   18 RCTs, N=3,170, not the headline N=110,594).
4. **Three contradictory claims about which idea is most crowded**, none matching the source's
   ranking.
5. **HumanAgencyBench's finding upgraded** from a training-design tension into a cross-model
   correlation the source never reports.
6. **psychosis-bench's mean scores converted into invented frequencies** ("far more often",
   "about a third of the time") — in a document whose own caveat says every number is
   second-hand.
7. **The recommendation's one-point margin stated without its cause** — it is one contestable
   scoring cell, and the source explicitly invites the team to overturn it.
8. **"Genuinely nobody has done it"** — the one phrase the brief's own stated rule forbids.
9. **Avenue 6A offered as a viable project** when the source says it scores joint-last and is
   a write-up frame.
10. **DM given only the reasons against their proposal**, omitting that the search found the
    Socratic + CBT/DBT hybrid genuinely unbuilt.
11. **DM's quote truncated** inside quotation marks without an ellipsis.
12. **Vaccaro's decisive moderator dropped** — combination helps when the human is better and
    hurts when the AI is.
13. "Two of AH's bullets **flagged as unsupported**" — one was unsupported, one overstated.
14. **Week 1 silently dropped reading gates** from the source's list.
15. Precision slips: "four critique rounds" (two rounds, four documents); "~200 papers"
    (~150); "biggest benchmark"; "93% accuracy" (macro-F1); "three links" (four); missing 3A
    ethics note; preprints unflagged.

**Readability problems (all fixed):** the glossary defined *sycophancy* and *effect size*,
neither used in the body, while omitting **IRB** — the most consequential thing the brief asks
anyone to do — plus API, LLM, arXiv ID and "annotation"; self-criticism was front-loaded under
a heading telling readers not to trust the document; week 1 was an unusable wall of italic
names and bare numbers; non-technical readers were told what they could do exactly once, in a
table cell at 74% depth; "could kill it" was buried in a headerless table; "my own recommended
study design was underpowered" was ambiguous (it was 4A, not the recommended 3A).

**Omissions (all closed):** capacity anchor; that the standing assumptions are inferred;
"do not run studies with people in mental-health crisis" and don't publish raw generations;
AH's peer-reviewed version to cite; pick a venue and deadline in week 2; pilots are not free;
Idea 5's feasibility flag; "2A+4A is not de-risking"; 3B as a first-class avenue; no signature.

## Round 2 — faithfulness 9/10, readability 8/10

**All 15 faithfulness items verified fixed**, with no drift introduced by the rewrite. The
critic noted the brief's *Choose Your Agent* description had become **more accurate than the
literature review's own §4.4.2**, which still carried the stale wording — now also fixed.

**Five new problems, all fixed:** heading said "nine papers" over a ten-row table; "two more
reading checks" was one, and the other was not a reading check; the reading assignment covered
six of ten papers; an arXiv rebuttal called "published" in a document whose glossary
distinguishes preprints; **avenue codes dropped**, so "argue with the cells" pointed at a
table the reader could not navigate.

**Remaining gaps, all closed:** Idea 5's nearest competitor (arXiv:2603.01341) unnamed; 2A's
Phase 1.5 expert-rated fallback unnamed; the ranking contingency DM can personally move
(endorsing the reframe takes 4A's buy-in cell from 2 to 5) unstated; Idea 4's heading missing
DM's initials.

**Two style items:** duplicate "3 —" row labels (fixed), and bold density essentially
unchanged from round 1 (trimmed, heaviest in the recommendation section where safety rules had
carried the same weight as parentheticals).

**On length**, the critic's judgement: *"~4,000 words is at the ceiling… but the 32% growth is
almost entirely the ten round-1 omissions being filled, and the one-paragraph version up top
means a low-technical reader can stop after 155 words. I would not cut content; I'd cut bold."*

## What the critic said to preserve

- One answer to "which project," in paragraph two, with effort, participants and cost
  attached — *"the source documents gave four different answers past 90% depth; this fixes
  their worst structural flaw."*
- The trust rule: *"when I say 'nobody has done X,' that means 'two searches didn't find X.'
  Treat it as a lead, not a fact."*
- The npj row's *"**not** evidence that effects shrank"* — a caveat almost any summary drops.
- The AH citation question's two-branch structure, and retracting a public correction of a
  teammate, which *"reads as generous rather than grovelling."*
- The DM attribution box — *"That may be the right call, but it should be yours"* — and the
  CBT/DBT-as-a-mode compromise.
- Idea 6's caveat that nobody put their initials on it, *"which says more about the meeting
  than about the idea."*
- The per-idea "What exists / What's still open / Honest correction" rhythm, with corrections
  sitting next to the claims they correct rather than in a footnote.
